The opportunity

From fragmented signals to a connected fan experience

Most sports organizations already have valuable fan data, but it is fragmented across ticketing, loyalty, mobile apps, point of sale, parking, venue operations, and game systems. That makes it difficult to recognize a fan's current context or respond while the moment still matters.

Sports-IQ creates a shared understanding of the fan and the game-day journey. It combines what is happening now with what the organization already knows, then uses trusted venue, policy, loyalty, weather, and journey knowledge to recommend a relevant and feasible next action.

UnderstandThe fan, history, and current situation.
DecideWhether assistance, an offer, or silence is best.
ActWhile the opportunity is still relevant.
MeasureThe fan and commercial outcome.
LearnWhat should happen differently next time.

Six outcome plays

Lead with value, then reveal the foundation

Each play connects a business result to the experiences, prerequisites, measures, and stakeholders required to make it real.

1

Know Your Fans and Create Hyper-Personalized Offers

Increase spend and conversion by making each offer relevant to the fan, game, and moment. +

Instead of sending the same halftime promotion to everyone, recognize that one fan usually buys food in the second quarter, another responds to merchandise after a win, and another should not receive an offer because they recently redeemed one.

What becomes possible

  • Match live behavior with loyalty, purchases, preferences, seat, and offer history.
  • Detect when a fan is behind their normal purchase or engagement pattern.
  • Select an offer the fan is likely to value and can realistically use.
  • Personalize timing, category, value, message, and channel.
  • Attribute redemption and revenue to the originating decision.

Prerequisites

  • Unify live fan signals and analytical history.
  • Resolve consented identity across ticketing, app, loyalty, and POS.
  • Connect fan and game identifiers to the current session.
  • Capture delivery, redemption, transaction, and non-response.
  • Use controls to distinguish incremental value.

Measures

  • Incremental revenue and contribution margin per addressable fan
  • Conversion and redemption lift versus control
  • Average transaction value
  • Cost per incremental redemption
  • Offer fatigue and opt-out rate
Buyer conversation: Chief Revenue Officer, Chief Marketing Officer, Head of Loyalty
2

Protect the Live Moment While Growing Revenue

Increase commercial effectiveness without degrading the experience fans came to enjoy. +

The next best action is sometimes no action. A fan watching a critical play, celebrating a score, or showing high engagement should not be interrupted. Sports-IQ identifies natural pauses and suppresses commercial messages during peak excitement.

What becomes possible

  • Recognize Red Zone drives, clutch time, power plays, VAR reviews, and intermissions.
  • Reserve engagement for timeouts, breaks, and arrival windows.
  • Apply loyalty-aware contact limits, budgets, and responsible-offer policies.
  • Coordinate service messages and promotions.

Prerequisites

  • Stream reliable game-state and engagement signals.
  • Define when outreach is allowed, discouraged, or prohibited.
  • Agree frequency, eligibility, budget, alcohol, and safety policies.
  • Monitor latency so actions do not arrive after the moment.

Measures

  • Revenue or margin per message
  • Conversion by game moment
  • Dismissal and opt-out rates
  • Engagement recovery
  • Decision-to-delivery latency
Buyer conversation: Chief Experience Officer, Chief Revenue Officer, Digital Product Leader
3

Make Every Recommendation Relevant and Feasible

Replace generic recommendations with actions grounded in the realities of the venue. +

A relevant offer is still a poor experience if the item is unavailable, the concession is too far away, the queue is too long, or the fan is exposed to bad weather. Sports-IQ combines fan intent with venue and operational knowledge.

What becomes possible

  • Recommend the nearest suitable concession or merchandise location.
  • Account for walk time, accessibility, weather, menu, and preparation time.
  • Match loyalty benefits to entitlement and context.
  • Adapt when a zone is closed, congested, or at capacity.
  • Prioritize mobile ordering, seat delivery, or weather-aware items.

Prerequisites

  • Curate venue guides, maps, menus, loyalty rules, and policies.
  • Connect current weather and operational status.
  • Assign content ownership and freshness standards.
  • Require grounded answers and preserve traceability.

Measures

  • Fulfillment and redemption rate
  • Abandoned order rate
  • Average walk and wait time
  • Recommendation accuracy
  • Grounding and policy compliance
Buyer conversation: Chief Operating Officer, Venue Operations Leader, Head of Guest Experience
4

Remove Friction From Doorstep to Seat and Back

Make game day easier before the fan enters the venue and after the event ends. +

The fan experience starts before arrival. Sports-IQ can evolve into a journey companion that helps fans plan when to leave, choose a route, find parking, reach the right gate, navigate the venue, and plan the trip home.

What becomes possible

  • Combine ticket, venue, weather, traffic, transit, and parking context.
  • Recommend parking by gate, accessibility, arrival, and availability.
  • Adapt route and departure guidance to current conditions.
  • Guide fans from parking or transit to their seats.
  • Recommend the least-friction return route.

Prerequisites

  • Connect trusted traffic, transit, closure, and parking context.
  • Maintain authoritative gate, accessibility, and parking knowledge.
  • Obtain consent for location-aware assistance.
  • Define handoffs to mapping, parking, ticketing, and team apps.
  • Design fallbacks for unavailable external data.

Measures

  • On-time arrival and gate entry
  • Parking-assistance completion
  • Time from perimeter to seat
  • Game-day support contacts
  • Fan satisfaction and app retention
  • Pre-game service revenue
Buyer conversation: Chief Operating Officer, Chief Experience Officer, Head of Digital
5

Turn Every Interaction Into a Learning Loop

Build a measurable system that improves fan decisions instead of running disconnected campaigns. +

Each recommendation should produce evidence: Was it delivered? Did the fan engage? Was there a transaction? Did it improve the experience or create fatigue? Sports-IQ connects actions to outcomes so teams continuously improve.

What becomes possible

  • Link decisions to delivery, response, redemption, and transactions.
  • Compare performance by segment, zone, channel, and moment.
  • Identify incremental margin rather than attributed gross sales.
  • Update preferences and decision policies from observed behavior.
  • Share performance across commercial, experience, and operations teams.

Prerequisites

  • Create a common fan, game, offer, and transaction measurement model.
  • Preserve correlation identifiers through fulfillment.
  • Establish holdout groups and experimentation governance.
  • Separate gross revenue, incremental revenue, margin, and cost.

Measures

  • Incremental contribution margin versus control
  • Attributable redemption
  • Decision precision and acceptance
  • Repeat engagement
  • Lifetime value and retention indicators
  • Cost-to-value ratio
Buyer conversation: Chief Data Officer, Chief Revenue Officer, Finance Leader
6

Give Teams a Shared Game-Day Operating Picture

Help revenue, guest experience, loyalty, and venue operations act from the same current understanding. +

The same intelligence used for fan decisions can answer operational questions: Which sections show declining engagement? Which concession zones are active? Where is revenue at risk? This shortens the distance between insight and action.

What becomes possible

  • Ask business questions across live and historical context.
  • Detect demand, congestion, service, or engagement patterns.
  • Give human operators context before sensitive actions.
  • Create one view from live operations through commercial outcomes.

Prerequisites

  • Standardize fan, game, venue, offer, and purchase definitions.
  • Apply role-based access and protect personal information.
  • Publish trusted measures and authoritative sources.
  • Capture operator decisions in the learning loop.

Measures

  • Time from question to decision
  • Manual analysis hours avoided
  • Intervention completion
  • Operational response time
  • Adoption by revenue and venue teams
Buyer conversation: Chief Operating Officer, Chief Data Officer, Revenue Operations Leader

Discovery questions

Find the first outcome worth proving

Business Value

  • Which game-day revenue streams have the largest gap between fan intent and conversion?
  • Where do generic campaigns create waste or fan fatigue?
  • Which journeys have measurable outcomes for a controlled pilot?
  • How is incremental value distinguished from attributed sales?

Fan Experience

  • Where do fans experience the most friction before, during, and after an event?
  • Which moments should never be interrupted?
  • Which loyalty benefits are difficult to discover or redeem?
  • What consent and communication preferences are captured?

Data and Operations

  • Can ticketing, app, loyalty, POS, and parking resolve to one consented fan?
  • How quickly are game, transaction, queue, and inventory signals available?
  • Who owns venue, menu, parking, loyalty, and freshness?
  • Can an offer be traced through delivery, redemption, and transaction?

Scale and Governance

  • Which policies require human approval or deterministic enforcement?
  • What are acceptable latency, failure, and fallback thresholds?
  • Which metrics must improve before expansion?
  • How will commercial value and fan trust be reviewed together?

60-second executive pitch

Turn game-day context into timely fan value

Sports organizations have only a few seconds to act on what is happening during a live event, but fan, transaction, venue, and game information is usually fragmented. Sports-IQ connects those signals with each fan's loyalty, spending, purchase, and response history to determine whether the next best action is useful guidance, a relevant offer, or no interruption at all.

It protects peak moments by suppressing outreach when fans are highly engaged. At the right moments, it can recommend an offer grounded in real venue constraints, help a fan find the best concession, adapt to weather, or guide their route to the stadium, parking, gate, seat, and trip home. Every action can be connected to a measurable response, creating a learning loop across revenue, experience, and operations.

The recommended entry point is a controlled pilot around one observable fan journey, measured for incremental contribution margin, fan response, and decision speed. Once proven, the same foundation can expand across the complete game-day experience.
